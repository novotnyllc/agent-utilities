# What failed, and why

Methods tried on a foil-spandex bodysuit with printed-tulle panels, in the order
they were abandoned. Read this before suggesting any of them again.

## Glue and fusibles

| Method | Result | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E6000 Plus | Failed pull test | Does not grip the slick foil face |
| Hot glue | Sticks, but fails | Pulls the foil off its knit |
| UV resin | Sticks, but fails | Pulls the foil off its knit |
| Iron-on fusible | Not possible | Foil can't be ironed |

On plain lycra (no foil), a stretch fusible can work as a baste. Stitch over it
anyway.

## Triple straight-stretch stitch

The forward-back-forward stitch looked right for stretch fabric, but on foil the
feed dogs only stretched the fabric back and forth. The needle kept landing in one
spot, knotting the thread and perforating the foil.

Did not help:

- lowering presser foot pressure (a too-low setting jammed outright);
- paper under or over the fabric;
- tape on the foot sole;
- maximum stitch length (3.0 on that machine).

A walking foot is not a fix either: it is not designed for reverse-feeding
stitches.

Plain zigzag fed cleanly with the same setup.

## Sewing the panels onto a finished tube

Working a closed bodysuit on a free arm meant only edges running around the body
fed correctly. Side-seam edges pointed the wrong way. Opening one side seam,
sewing flat and re-serging was faster than every workaround.

## Hand-tacking every edge

Three tiny stitches in one spot every 1–2 in (25–50 mm), thread travelling under
the mesh between tacks:

- over an hour for half a shoulder;
- tiny targets, very hard with poor eyesight;
- on a tight spandex garment on a form, the needle went through the whole layer;
- knots pulled through the mesh holes.

Overcasting the edge with a fine needle is the least bad hand method, and only for
short stretches.

## Margins

¼ in (6 mm) of bare mesh round the print left no room for the foot or the zigzag
swing. ⅜–½ in (10–13 mm) is the minimum. Panels printed right up to armholes,
necklines and side seams could not be stitched at those edges at all.

## Time

Every estimate based on sewing normal woven or knit fabric was off by several
times. Plan the schedule from the scrap test, not from experience with other
fabrics.
